Waterproof Ratings Explained

Jacket labels are full of numbers: 10,000 mm, 20K/20K, 2.5L, fully taped seams. Here is what they mean and which ones matter for the weather you actually get.

01.1

Hydrostatic head

The waterproof rating, in millimetres, comes from a lab test: a tube of water is placed on the fabric and filled until drops come through. A 5,000 mm fabric handles light rain; 10,000 mm suits most everyday rain; 20,000 mm and above is for long, heavy or wind-driven storms.

01.2

Breathability

Breathability is usually given in grams of water vapour per square metre per day. Higher numbers let more sweat escape. Around 10,000 g is fine for walking; 20,000 g or more helps when you are working hard, such as cycling uphill or hiking with a pack.

01.3

Layers and construction

  • 2-layer: membrane bonded to the outer fabric, with a loose hanging lining. Comfortable and affordable.
  • 2.5-layer: a printed protective coating instead of a lining. Light and packable.
  • 3-layer: outer, membrane and lining bonded together. Toughest and most breathable.
01.4

Seams and zips

Every stitch is a tiny hole. Fully taped seams are sealed inside with waterproof tape. Look for water-resistant zips or storm flaps over the zip, and a hood with a stiff brim and adjusters so it moves with your head.

01.5

Water-resistant vs waterproof

A water-resistant jacket sheds light showers thanks to its finish but will soak through in steady rain. A waterproof jacket has a membrane or coating plus taped seams. For short city trips, water-resistant can be enough.
